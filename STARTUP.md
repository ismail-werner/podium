# Podium: startup plan

## The honest starting point
Right now Podium is a nicely designed reader for other publishers' RSS feeds. That is not a business yet:
- Anyone can copy it, and Google News and Feedly already do it.
- Many publishers' feed terms allow personal or non-commercial use only. Putting ads next to their headlines and images can break those terms. Check each source before you earn money from it.

So the plan is to add something that is yours.

## Why Formula 1 and tennis
Both have large, loyal, global audiences, long seasons, and fans who spend money (tickets, merchandise, streaming, betting, travel). Each has big spikes of interest around races and tournaments.

## Pick one angle (don't do all of them)
1. **Race-weekend and tournament briefings** (recommended start). A short email or page before each Grand Prix and Grand Slam: what to watch, who is in form, in your own words, linking to the sources. Opinion and summary are yours, so there is no copyright problem. Costs nothing to start.
2. **A niche audience.** One team, one driver or one tour only (for example the ATP tour, or a single team's fans). Small audiences are loyal and easier to rank for.
3. **A tool, not just news.** A race and match calendar with local times, a "should I stay up for this" guide, a follow-your-favorites alert. Tools get returning visitors.
4. **A language gap.** Quality F1 and tennis coverage in French or Arabic, if that is your audience.

## How it earns money (in order of effort)
| Stage | Method | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Affiliate links**: tickets, streaming passes, merchandise, travel booking | Some traffic, no inventory |
| 2 | **Display ads** (Google AdSense, then Ezoic or Mediavine) | Original content and about 10k visits a month |
| 3 | **Newsletter** with a sponsor slot | 1,000+ subscribers |
| 4 | **Sponsored posts** from brands, clearly labelled | A real audience |
| 5 | **Pro tier**: ad-free, alerts, saved lists | Accounts and a backend |

Realistic expectation: the first months earn close to nothing. Income comes from consistent traffic, usually after 6 to 12 months.

## 90-day plan
- **Weeks 1-2:** Buy a domain, put the site online (GitHub Pages, Cloudflare Pages or Netlify, free). Add an About page, Privacy page and Contact page. These are needed for ad networks.
- **Weeks 3-6:** Pick your angle. Publish your own short posts around each race weekend and tournament. Add a newsletter signup (Buttondown or Substack free tier).
- **Weeks 7-10:** Add affiliate links. Post on Reddit, X and Instagram where fans already are.
- **Weeks 11-13:** Apply for AdSense. Check what people clicked, then double down on that.

## Tech to-do (when you are ready)
- Small backend that fetches the feeds on a schedule, so you stop depending on rss2json's free limit.
- A database for stories, so posts have stable URLs. Good for search engines.
- Your own post editor, so you can write articles in the same design.
- Accounts and saved stories on the server (today they live only in the browser).

## Legal checklist
- Show only headline, a short excerpt and a link back. Never copy full articles or reuse images without permission. Switch to your own images or licensed ones when you monetize.
- Label sponsored posts and affiliate links.
- Add a privacy policy and cookie notice (needed for ads, especially for EU visitors).
- Do not use official logos, driver or player photos, or names like "Formula 1" or "Wimbledon" in your brand. Mentioning them in headlines is fine.

## First thing to do this week
Decide your angle, then write the briefing for the next race weekend or tournament. Everything else builds on that.
